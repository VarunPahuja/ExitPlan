# Immigration Policy Briefs — Germany & Ireland

*Compiled May 2026. Immigration rules change frequently —
verify all figures against official sources before applying.*

---

## Germany (DE)

### EU Blue Card (§18g AufenthG)

The EU Blue Card is Germany's primary route for non-EU
skilled workers with a university degree. The 2026 minimum
gross annual salary threshold is approximately **€45,300**
for most occupations, reduced to approximately **€41,041**
for shortage occupations (IT, engineering, mathematics,
natural sciences). Thresholds are adjusted annually by
the Federal Ministry of the Interior — verify the current
year's figure before applying. A concrete job offer is
required before applying. The Blue Card is initially
issued for 4 years or the contract duration plus 3 months.
Holders may change employers freely after the first 12
months; during the first 12 months, the foreigners
authority must be notified of any job change.
Source: bamf.de, bundesregierung.de.

### Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)

Germany introduced the Opportunity Card in June 2024
under the Skilled Immigration Act. It allows qualified
workers to enter Germany for up to 1 year to look for
work without needing a job offer first. Eligibility
requires either a German-recognized qualification or a
foreign qualification plus a points score of at least
6 out of 10. Points awarded for: German language skills
(up to 3 points), qualified professional experience
(1-2 points), age under 35 (1 point), previous stays
in Germany (1 point), and a spouse with a recognized
qualification (1 point). Holders can work up to 20 hours
per week in any job while searching.
Source: bamf.de.

### Permanent Settlement Permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis)

There are two distinct routes depending on permit type:

**EU Blue Card route:** Holders can apply for the permanent
settlement permit after **21 months** with B1 German
language skills, or **27 months** with A1 German (minimum).
This is among the fastest PR routes in the EU.

**Standard skilled worker route (without Blue Card):**
Requires **4 years** of continuous employment on a
temporary residence permit. Applicants must also
demonstrate: secure subsistence without public funds,
at least **60 months** of statutory pension contributions
(or equivalent), B1 German language skills, and adequate
housing. The 60-month pension contribution requirement
applies to the standard route — Blue Card holders on the
accelerated route are not subject to this requirement.

Time spent on student visas or job seeker visas does not
generally count toward either route.
Source: gesetze-im-internet.de §9, §18g AufenthG.

### Job Seeker Visa (§20 AufenthG)

Qualified professionals can enter Germany on a Job Seeker
Visa for up to **6 months** to find employment.
Requirements: a German or recognized foreign university
degree or vocational qualification, proof of sufficient
financial resources (approximately €1,100/month), and
health insurance. Holders may work up to 10 hours per
week in trial employment during the search period.
If a qualifying job offer is found, the holder can switch
to a work permit without leaving Germany.
Source: bamf.de.

### Language Requirements

German language requirements vary by pathway:
- EU Blue Card: no German required for the permit itself,
  but B1 required for the accelerated 21-month PR route
- Niederlassungserlaubnis (standard): B1 German minimum
- Approbation (medical license): B2/C1 German — this is
  a near-absolute barrier for Indian medical graduates
  without prior German study. Medical demand is real but
  practical accessibility for non-German speakers is low.
- Integration course: up to 700 hours of language and
  orientation instruction, required for some permit types.
Source: bamf.de.

### Citizenship and Dual Nationality

Germany's 2024 citizenship reform generally permits
multiple citizenships. However, India does not recognize
dual citizenship. Indian nationals who naturalize as
German citizens are generally required to surrender their
Indian citizenship and may subsequently apply for OCI
(Overseas Citizen of India) status.
Source: bundesregierung.de, mea.gov.in.

## Ireland (IE)

### Critical Skills Employment Permit (CSEP)

The Critical Skills Employment Permit is Ireland's primary
route for skilled non-EEA workers. The current salary
threshold is approximately **€41,700 per year** for
occupations on the Critical Skills Occupations List
(subject to annual review — verify the current threshold
before applying). The permit waives the Labour Market
Needs Test. Initial permit duration is **2 years**,
renewable. Eligible sectors include ICT, engineering,
healthcare, financial services, and research. Permit
holders can bring immediate family members — spouses
receive Stamp 1G, granting full work authorization
without needing their own employment permit.
Source: enterprise.gov.ie.

### Stamp 4 — Path to Employer Independence

After completing the 2-year Critical Skills Employment
Permit period (typically around 21-24 months in practice,
depending on permit dates and registration timing), holders
may apply to the Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) for
Stamp 4 permission. Stamp 4 grants the right to live and
work in Ireland without an employment permit — fully
employer-independent. It is valid for **5 years** and
renewable. Stamp 4 is the key milestone: once achieved,
the holder can change jobs freely, become self-employed,
or start a company without immigration restriction.
Source: irishimmigration.ie.

### Long-Term Residency and Citizenship

After **5 years** of continuous lawful residence,
non-EEA nationals can apply for Long-Term Residency —
indefinite permission to remain in Ireland. Irish
citizenship by naturalization requires **5 years** of
continuous residence in the 8 years prior to application,
with the final year being continuous. Time on a student
visa (Stamp 2) counts toward the citizenship residency
requirement.

Ireland permits dual citizenship. However, India does not
recognize dual citizenship. Indian nationals who naturalize
as Irish citizens are generally required to surrender their
Indian citizenship and may subsequently apply for OCI
(Overseas Citizen of India) status.
Source: irishimmigration.ie, citizensinformation.ie,
mea.gov.in.

### General Employment Permit (GEP)

The General Employment Permit covers roles not on the
Critical Skills list and requires a Labour Market Needs
Test. Salary minimum is approximately **€34,000 per year**
(subject to review). Duration is initially 2 years,
renewable for 3 more years. After 5 years continuous
employment on a GEP, the holder no longer requires an
employment permit. Spouses of GEP holders receive Stamp 3
— they can reside in Ireland but cannot work without their
own permit (unlike CSEP spouses who receive Stamp 1G).
Source: enterprise.gov.ie.

### GNIB Registration

All non-EEA nationals must register with the Garda
National Immigration Bureau (GNIB) — processed through
the Immigration Service Delivery online system — within
**90 days** of arriving in Ireland. Registration provides
an Irish Residence Permit (IRP) card, which is the primary
proof of lawful residence and required for banking, tax
registration, and social services. Registration must be
renewed annually or on permit renewal.
Source: irishimmigration.ie.

## Germany vs Ireland — Quick Comparison for Indian STEM Graduate

| Factor | Germany | Ireland |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Entry permit | EU Blue Card (job offer, ~€45,300 salary) | CSEP (job offer, ~€41,700 salary) |
| Employer independence | After 12 months on Blue Card | After ~2 years → Stamp 4 |
| PR timeline | 21 months (B1 German) or 27 months | 5 years Long-Term Residency |
| Language barrier | B1 German for fast PR; B2/C1 for medicine | None — English throughout |
| Family work rights | Spouse needs own permit | Spouse gets Stamp 1G (full work rights) |
| Dual citizenship | Germany permits it; India does not | Ireland permits it; India does not |
| Tech salary market | Strong (Berlin, Munich, Hamburg) | Strong (FAANG Dublin, IFSC finance) |

Germany offers faster permanent residence for Blue Card
holders (21-27 months). Ireland offers faster employer
independence (Stamp 4 after approximately 2 years).
For Indian CS graduates prioritizing PR speed, Germany
wins on timeline. For those prioritizing no language
barrier and family work rights, Ireland wins.
