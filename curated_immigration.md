# Country Immigration Policy Briefs

*For Indian international students. Compiled May 2026. Verify against official sources before relying on specific numbers — UK and Canada in particular have had multiple rule changes in 2024–25.*

---

## Singapore (SG)

### Employment Pass (EP)

The Employment Pass requires a minimum monthly salary of **S$5,600** for most sectors and **S$6,200** for financial services, in effect since 1 January 2025 and applied to renewals from 1 January 2026. MOM has confirmed thresholds rise again to **S$6,000 / S$6,600** on 1 January 2027. Applications are assessed via **COMPASS** (Complementarity Assessment Framework): a points framework scoring candidates across four foundational criteria (salary vs sector benchmark, qualifications, diversity contribution, support for local employment) and two bonus criteria (skills bonus, strategic economic priorities bonus). Candidates need **40 points minimum** to pass, with 10 or 20 points awarded per criterion. Processing typically takes **3 weeks** for new applications; approval rate is approximately **80%+** for well-qualified candidates at established employers. EP holders earning S$6,000+/month can sponsor a spouse and children under 21 on Dependant's Passes. Source: mom.gov.sg.

### S Pass

The S Pass is for mid-skilled foreign workers and requires a minimum monthly salary of **S$3,150** (S$3,650 for financial services), with both rising in tiered increments. Unlike the EP, the S Pass is subject to a strict **quota and levy system**: services sector employers can have S Pass holders make up no more than **10% of total workforce**, with other sectors capped at 15–18%. Employers pay a monthly levy of **S$550 (Tier 1)** or **S$650 (Tier 2)** per S Pass holder, with Tier 2 applying once an employer crosses the basic quota. S Pass holders face higher scrutiny on PR applications, lower approval rates than EP holders, and quota instability that weakens long-term planning. Source: mom.gov.sg.

### Permanent Residence (PR)

PR applications are submitted to **ICA** (Immigration & Checkpoints Authority) under the **Professionals/Technical Personnel and Skilled Workers (PTS)** scheme. There is no fixed waiting period, but EP holders typically apply after **2+ years** of stable employment in Singapore. ICA does not publish a fixed annual quota, but following Budget 2026, the government indicated PR intake will rise to approximately **40,000 approvals per year** over the next five years, up from ~35,000 recently. Approval is discretionary and holistic — ICA considers salary level relative to age and sector benchmarks, employment continuity, tax contribution history, length of residence, family ties in Singapore, and economic contribution. ICA does not provide reasons for rejection. End-to-end timelines typically run **6 to 12 months**, though straightforward cases can clear in 4 months and complex ones can stretch beyond 12. Source: ica.gov.sg.

## Netherlands (NL)

### Highly Skilled Migrant (Kennismigrant)

The Kennismigrant permit is the Netherlands' primary route for non-EU skilled workers and is **employer-sponsored** — the employer must be an IND-recognised sponsor. As of **1 January 2026**, the gross monthly salary thresholds (excluding 8% holiday allowance) are: **€5,942** for applicants aged 30 and over, **€4,357** for applicants under 30, and **€3,122** under the reduced rate for recent graduates of a Dutch master's/PhD or who completed a search year. Certain scientists, researchers, and medical specialists in training are automatically eligible regardless of salary. Processing time is **2 to 4 weeks** — among the fastest skilled-migrant tracks in Europe. Permits are issued for the contract duration up to 5 years and are renewable. Spouses and registered partners receive a residence permit with **unrestricted work authorization**. Source: ind.nl/en.

### 30% Tax Ruling

The 30% ruling allows qualifying highly skilled migrants recruited from abroad to receive **30% of gross salary tax-free** for up to 5 years. To qualify, the employee must have lived more than **150 km from the Dutch border for at least 16 of the 24 months** before employment start, and taxable salary must exceed **€46,107** (or **€35,048** for those under 30 with a recognised master's degree) in 2026 — slightly above the Kennismigrant thresholds. The ruling will be **scaled back to 27% from 2027** under legislation already passed. The ruling effectively reduces income tax on a senior tech salary by roughly **8–10 percentage points** during the five-year window, which is the single largest financial driver of Netherlands' attractiveness for skilled non-EU migrants. Source: belastingdienst.nl.

### EU Blue Card

The Netherlands implements the EU Blue Card alongside the national Kennismigrant route. For 2026, the EU Blue Card salary threshold is **€5,867 gross/month** (roughly the same as the Kennismigrant 30+ threshold). The Blue Card requires a recognised **bachelor's degree or higher**, or **5+ years of professional experience** in IT/ICT occupations as defined in the EU directive. The Blue Card's main advantage over Kennismigrant is **intra-EU mobility** — after 12 months in the Netherlands, holders can move to another EU member state and apply for a Blue Card there with simplified procedures. For most Indian applicants whose employer is Netherlands-based, the Kennismigrant route is faster and equally good; the Blue Card is mainly useful if EU mobility is a goal. Source: ind.nl/en.

### Permanent Residence (Verblijfsvergunning voor onbepaalde tijd)

After **5 continuous years** of legal residence on a residence permit with a non-temporary purpose, applicants can apply for Dutch permanent residence. **Time on the Kennismigrant permit counts directly** toward the 5-year requirement. Requirements: income at or above the Kennismigrant threshold at time of application, passing the **civic integration exam (inburgeringsexamen) at A2 level**, no criminal record, and continuous legal residence with no absences over 6 months at a time (or 4 of any 6-year period). Application fee is €207 in 2026. Dutch citizenship becomes available after the same 5 years subject to the same A2 exam and renunciation of prior nationality (with limited exceptions — India does not currently grant dual citizenship, so Indian applicants must surrender Indian citizenship to naturalise). Source: ind.nl/en.

## Canada (CA)

### Express Entry & CRS

Express Entry manages applications for three federal economic programs: **Canadian Experience Class (CEC)**, **Federal Skilled Worker (FSW)**, and **Federal Skilled Trades (FST)**. Candidates create a profile and are scored out of **1,200 points** using the **Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS)** — based on age (up to 110), education (up to 150), language (up to 260 across English/French), work experience (up to 80), skill transferability (up to 100), and additional factors including a provincial nomination (600 points, effectively guaranteeing an ITA). **Job offer points were removed in March 2025** — candidates no longer get 50-200 CRS points for a valid Canadian job offer, a major change that disadvantaged many tech workers. **Upfront medical exams** have been required since August 2025. Processing time after ITA: ~**7 months** (IRCC service standard is 6 months for 80% of cases); total pool-entry to PR is typically **9–18 months** depending on stream. The 2025–2027 Levels Plan reduced PR targets to **380,000 in 2026** and 365,000 in 2027, pushing general draw cutoffs higher. Source: canada.ca/immigration.

### Category-Based Draws (2026 cutoffs)

In 2025, **98% of all ITAs** were issued through category-based draws rather than general rounds. For 2026, IRCC has **10 active categories**: French-language proficiency, healthcare & social services, STEM, trades, education, physicians, researchers, senior managers, transport workers, and skilled military recruits. **Recent CRS cutoffs (2025–early 2026)**: French speakers 379–393, trades 433–440, education 479, healthcare 463–507, STEM 481–507, general FSW 485–547, CEC 504–535, Provincial Nominee enhanced 727–803 (due to 600-point bonus). Note: the **STEM category has been dormant for over 23 months** — STEM candidates should not rely on it returning and should plan via PNP or CEC instead. From February 2026, category-based draws require **12 months of full-time work experience** in the eligible occupation (up from 6 months previously), which is expected to reduce competition and lower cutoffs for those who qualify. Source: canada.ca/immigration.

### Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

The PGWP is an **open work permit** (no employer sponsorship needed) for graduates of eligible Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs). Duration depends on program length: **8 months to under 2 years → PGWP of same duration**; **2+ year programs → 3-year PGWP**; **master's degree of any length → 3-year PGWP**. As of **1 November 2024**, most applicants must provide **language test results**: CLB 7 for university graduates, CLB 5 for college/non-degree graduates. **Field-of-study restrictions** apply to non-degree (college/diploma) graduates — the program must have an eligible **Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) code** linked to a long-term labour shortage. **Bachelor's, master's, and PhD graduates remain exempt** from field-of-study restrictions. IRCC **froze the PGWP-eligible field-of-study list on 15 January 2026** — no additions or removals through 2026. Applicants must apply within **180 days** of receiving final marks confirming program completion. Source: canada.ca/immigration.

### Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) for Tech/Engineering

A provincial nomination adds **600 CRS points** — effectively a guaranteed ITA. The strongest provinces for tech and engineering are: **Ontario** (Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program — Human Capital Priorities and Employer Job Offer streams; Toronto/Waterloo tech corridor), **British Columbia** (BC PNP Tech, with weekly tech-specific draws targeting in-demand occupations like software developers, data scientists, web designers, and tech sales — generally lower thresholds than non-tech streams), **Alberta** (Alberta Advantage Immigration Program with Accelerated Tech Pathway), and **Saskatchewan** (SINP for engineers and IT roles, often with lower cutoffs than larger provinces). **Atlantic provinces** (NS, NB, NL, PEI) use the Atlantic Immigration Program for employer-sponsored direct PR. PNP enhanced (Express Entry-linked) processing is ~**7 months**; base PNP is ~**14 months**. Source: canada.ca/immigration and individual provincial nominee websites.

## United Kingdom (GB)

### Skilled Worker Visa

The Skilled Worker visa requires an offer from a Home Office **licensed sponsor**. Salary thresholds rose substantially on **22 July 2025**: the general minimum is now **£41,700 per year** (up from £38,700) or the occupation's "going rate" — **whichever is higher**. Lower thresholds apply for: **new entrants and shortage occupations at £33,400** (the Immigration Salary List, which gives an 80% discount on the general threshold but **not** on going rates), and **PhD holders in STEM roles at £37,500**. The **skill threshold moved back to RQF Level 6** (bachelor's degree-equivalent) on 22 July 2025, removing approximately **180 occupations at RQF Levels 3–5** from Skilled Worker eligibility. Sub-degree roles only remain eligible if on the **Immigration Salary List (ISL)** or the new **Temporary Shortage List (TSL)** — both expire 31 December 2026 pending the MAC's Stage 2 review (due July 2026). The points system requires **70 points total** from mandatory criteria (sponsor + appropriate skill level + English at B1 + salary at threshold). Workers on TSL/ISL roles below RQF 6 **cannot bring dependants**. Care worker route closed to overseas recruitment from 22 July 2025. Source: gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa.

### Graduate Route

The Graduate Route is an **unsponsored open work permit** for international students who have completed an eligible UK degree. Duration: **18 months** for bachelor's and master's graduates (reduced from 2 years for new applicants under proposals in the May 2025 white paper but the 2-year duration is still current as of May 2026 for those graduating before any rule change), **3 years for PhD graduates**. No salary threshold, no sponsorship required, no skill-level test — holders can work for any employer at any level, be self-employed, or look for work. **Time on the Graduate Route does not count toward ILR** (settlement), so most graduates use it as a bridge to a Skilled Worker sponsorship. Application must be made before the student visa expires, and the applicant must be in the UK at the time of application. Fee is **£880** plus the **Immigration Health Surcharge of £1,035/year**. Source: gov.uk/graduate-visa.

### Global Talent Visa

The Global Talent visa is for proven or promising leaders in **academia, research, arts and culture, or digital technology** — and unlike Skilled Worker, **does not require a job offer or sponsor**. Applicants need endorsement from an approved body: **The Royal Society** (sciences and medicine), **The British Academy** (humanities and social sciences), **The Royal Academy of Engineering** (engineering), **UK Research and Innovation (UKRI)** (research fellowships), **Tech Nation** (digital technology — until its endorsement function was transferred to the Home Office in mid-2024, now administered directly), or **Arts Council England** (arts and culture). Two categories: **Exceptional Talent** (already a recognised leader) or **Exceptional Promise** (emerging leader, typically early-career). Holders can switch employers freely, be self-employed, and bring dependants. **Settlement (ILR) after 3 years** for Exceptional Talent across all fields, and 3 years for Exceptional Promise in some categories (5 years for others) — significantly faster than the standard Skilled Worker 5-year route. Source: gov.uk/global-talent.

### Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) — Important 2026 Context

The current ILR rule is **5 years of continuous lawful residence** for Skilled Worker holders. However, the May 2025 white paper "Restoring Control over the Immigration System" proposed extending this to **10 years** under a new **"earned settlement"** framework, where reductions could be earned through income, employment continuity, English level, and integration. A government consultation closed February 2026; implementation is expected **late 2026 or 2027**. It remains unclear whether the change will apply retroactively to those already in the UK. Indian students planning a UK path should treat the 5-year route as uncertain and assume worst-case 10-year if entering the UK in 2026 or later. Source: gov.uk and Migration Observatory (Oxford).

## Cross-Country Summary for Indian Students

| Country | Fastest PR/Long-Term Path | Best-Fit Fields | Biggest Risk Factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore | ~2 yrs EP → PR (discretionary) | Finance, CS, Data Science | PR not guaranteed; rising salary floor |
| Netherlands | 5 yrs Kennismigrant → PR | CS, Engineering, Data Science | A2 Dutch for PR; housing crunch in Amsterdam |
| Canada | 9–18 months Express Entry → direct PR | CS, Engineering, Healthcare (STEM cat. dormant) | CRS cutoffs elevated; PR targets cut |
| United Kingdom | 5 yrs Skilled Worker → ILR (10 yrs proposed) | CS, Finance, Medicine | ILR reform uncertainty; £41,700 threshold |

*All figures verified against official sources for May 2026. Immigration rules change frequently — re-check before applying.*
